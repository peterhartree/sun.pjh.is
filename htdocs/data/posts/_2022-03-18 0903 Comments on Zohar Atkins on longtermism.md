# Comments on Zohar Atkins on longtermism


I guess I start with the thought that, whether we like it or not, at some point, the human version of Dasein will evolve into something "unrecognisable, categorically different". 

I imagine we have a limited ability to shape what that will look like and also some ability to speed up or slow down the process of change. 

Longtermism involves trying to understand and positively influence this process.

One might think that we can only think about things getting better or worse within a local bubble of meaning (e.g. the current human condition). It sounds like Heidegger and Zohar think something like this. If you try to think beyond your bubble, you get numbness, weirdness—not improvement or worsening, simply "change".

Maybe there are two options at that point: 

1. Trust the process, don't try to understand or intervene, let the next bubble emerge as it may (the religious option?).

2. Try to reason beyond your bubble via the principles you trust the most (e.g. "suffering bad"). Then try to influence the nature of the next bubble according to those principles.
