# Notes on the Pope on AI

_Style warning: written for myself, not readers..._

The Pope has a new [blog post](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html).

His notion of intelligence is too expansive. It blends many things I'd prefer to distinguish, e.g. optimisation power, consciousness, sentience, soul, spirit, wisdom, personhood, embodiment, empathy, divine connection. I think he's talking about "humanness" not "intelligence", so the conclusion that "AI systems lack human qualities" follows trivially.

I'm coming into all this from a very difference perspective: non-dualistic, evolutionary, Malthusian, etc. On that picture, the functionalist definition of intelligence points at the thing that matters most, even though it omits a bunch of nearby things we value. 

He underplays the similarities between how the human mind works and how AIs work. I guess that's natural if you're a dualist and reject computationalism. 

He's way overconfident that AI systems will not be moral patients, will not have conscious experience, and so on. Again, maybe this comes directly out of his dualism, but even people like David Chalmers (who maybe has the least-bad modern form of dualism) think that AIs are likely to be moral patients.

Like most people, his thinking is too anchored on current systems, not seeing ahead to (near) future developments. E.g. there are already lots of embodied AI systems with increasingly general forms of intelligence (Tesla, Waymo). There will be many more soon.

Generally he doesn't argue for the most cruxy claims, or even specify them clearly. E.g.

> By its very nature, human thought is a creative process that eludes programming and transcends constraints.

I guess he thinks there's loads of Christian doctrine that clarifies and backs up things like this, but outside of that realm I've not seen much to support these takes.

> Human dignity and the common good must never be violated for the sake of efficiency, for “technological developments that do not lead to an improvement in the quality of life of all humanity, but on the contrary, aggravate inequalities and conflicts, can never count as true progress.” Instead, AI should be put “at the service of another type of progress, one which is healthier, more human, more social, more integral.

I'm onboard with the vibe (even though we wouldn't agree on how to characterise human dignity). But I feel sad because Malthus says sorry you can only have nice things in some places and for so long. You get the artisanal strawberries during the bubbles of abundance, and then the surplus is competed away and Kaffivest becomes a Starbucks. Strong EMH is false, thankfully, so we do have scope to modulate these transitions. That's our locus of control. And here the Papal vibe is sometimes apt.
