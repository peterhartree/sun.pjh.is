# Will more intelligence make things better?

My last post asked: [What is AI alignment?](https://sun.pjh.is/what-is-ai-alignment)

A very different question is: will more intelligence make things better?

Tyler Cowen said recently:
> My core view: if we humans can get more intelligence into our hands, if we can’t turn that into to a positive… what is it we’re hoping for? This is our big chance! Let’s not blow it.

will the large-scale impacts of AI systems be positive or negative overall, according to your values?

One effect of AI systems will be to accelerate the growth of knowledge. 

You might think, like Tyler Cowen or David Deutsch, that more intelligence for humans nets out good—perhaps because there are far more "good actors" than "bad actors"; perhaps because you see complex systems as self-regulating; perhaps because you expect offence-defence balance to remain compatible with complex civilisation. On this sort of view, you might think that the balance nets out *overwhelmingly* positive, or you might think its more of a wash.

Alternatively: you might think that we live in a [Vulnerable World](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/vulnerable.pdf)—that there are black balls in the Urn of Discovery, that will reliably destroy the potential of civilisations like ours. If you think that, then we should perhaps slow down the engines of discovery until we get systems in place that can turn black balls grey, or reliably leave black balls in the Urn. The former seems easier than the latter. Folks like Cowen and Deutsch presumably think this do-able, while the more doom-y are more pessimistic.

A source of confusion: you might think that the civilisational changes required to turn black balls grey—or make grey balls off-white—would be dystopian. Perhaps, as Bostrom suggests, we need far stronger policing, surveillance and regulation of some technologies. You might think that the benefits of AI systems are not offset by the cost of changes such as these. Or, you might be [more sanguine about this kind of value drift](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/ai-fear-is-mostly-fear-of-future).
